## How this was researched, and its limits

This container's network policy blocks direct fetches from wikipedia.org, britannica.com,
tennisfame.com, atptour.com, tennis.com, the Grand Slam sites and commons.wikimedia.org. The only
research tool that worked was a **web search engine**. It returns result links plus a summary of
what those pages say. I could not open and read the pages myself. So every item below is tagged
with how well it holds up:

| Tag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| ✅ | Consistent across 2+ independent sources in the search results |
| ⚠️ | Only one source surfaced (often a Wikipedia draw page), so check the primary source before publishing |
| ❗ | Sources disagree, or the claim depends on how you count. Say so on the page |

"Wikipedia" below means the linked Wikipedia article as summarized by search. Where a primary source
exists (ATP results archive, Wimbledon draw PDF, ITHF), it's listed so you can verify it yourself.

## Family and childhood

- Father **Roy Laver**, a cattle rancher, was one of 13 children, all of whom played tennis. Mother
  **Melba (née Roffey)** was a tournament-level player. ✅ [Encyclopedia.com][enc], [ITHF][ithf]
- Roy and Melba met at a tennis tournament in the Queensland town of Dingo. The family always had a
  court beside the house. ⚠️ [Encyclopedia.com][enc]
- Siblings: older brothers **Trevor** and **Robert**, younger sister **Lois**. All played. ⚠️ [Encyclopedia.com][enc]
- Started at about **age six**, challenging his older brothers with a hand-me-down racquet cut down to
  fit. ✅ [Encyclopedia.com][enc], [ITHF][ithf]
- Early coach **Charlie Hollis**. Laver was then picked for a junior camp sponsored by the Brisbane
  *Courier-Mail* and run by **Harry Hopman**. ⚠️ [Encyclopedia.com][enc]
- Hollis reportedly told him: *"You'll never win Wimbledon with a slice backhand. You must learn to
  put topspin on the ball."* ⚠️ (the quote is attributed to Laver's own recollection) [EssentiallySports][es-quote]

## Turning pro and the lost years (1963–1967)

- Turned professional in **December 1962**, after helping Australia win the Davis Cup. Joined the pro
  troupe. Members of the players' association reportedly guaranteed him **US$110,000 over three
  years**. ⚠️ amount [JRank/Encyclopedia][jrank-pro]. ✅ timing [Wikipedia][wp], [SAHOF][sahof]
- As a pro he was **barred from the majors from 1963 to 1967 (21 majors)**. The amateur majors did not
  admit professionals until the Open Era. ✅ (seed fact; consistent with all sources)
- Rough start: in early 1963 **Lew Hoad won their first eight matches** and **Ken Rosewall won 11 of
  13**. By year's end, with three titles, Laver was No. 2 among pros behind Rosewall. ⚠️ [Wikipedia][wp], [SAHOF][sahof]
- **Pro "majors":** US Pro 1964, 1966, 1967. Wembley Pro 1964, 1965, 1966, 1967. French Pro 1967. That
  makes **8 pre-Open pro majors**, including a "Pro Slam" of all three in 1967. ⚠️ The "pro major" label
  is a later historians' convention, not an official one. ❗ Present it as that. [Wikipedia: pro majors][wp-promajors]
- **Wimbledon Pro, 25–28 August 1967:** the first event at Wimbledon open to male professionals since
  1930, and the only pro event ever held on Centre Court. Laver d. Rosewall **6–2, 6–2, 12–10** in the
  final. It is widely described as having paved the way for Open tennis. ⚠️ [Wikipedia: Wimbledon Pro][wp-wpro]
- ❗ Some sources say Laver won a record **18 titles in 1967**. Others give **18 titles in 1969**. Both
  may be true, but it's unconfirmed. Don't publish either without a primary check.

## The Open Era arrives (1968)

- Open Era began **22 April 1968** at the British Hard Court Championships, West Hants Club,
  Bournemouth. ✅ [ATP Heritage][atp-bournemouth], [Tennis.com][tc-bournemouth], [ITHF blog][ithf-open]
- **1968 French Open** (first Open-era major): **lost** final to Ken Rosewall 6–3, 6–1, 2–6, 6–2. ✅ [Tennis Majors][tm-1968f], [Wikipedia][wp-1968f]
- **1968 Wimbledon (major #7):** first Open Wimbledon. d. Tony Roche **6–3, 6–4, 6–2**. ✅ [Wikipedia][wp-1968w], [ITHF][ithf]
- 1968 Wimbledon men's champion's prize: **£2,000** (about US$2,621 then). ✅ [CNBC][cnbc-1968]

## Later career (1970–1976)

- **1971: first tennis player to pass US$1 million** in career prize money, helped by **$292,717** in
  1971 alone. ✅ [Britannica via search][brit], [EBSCO][ebsco], [Encyclopedia.com][enc]
- **WCT Finals, Dallas:** lost both finals to Rosewall.
  - 1971: 6–4, 1–6, 7–6(3), 7–6(4). ⚠️ [Wikipedia][wp-wct71]
  - 1972: 4–6, 6–0, 6–3, 6–7(3), 7–6(5). Called "the match that made tennis in the United States". ❗
    Its US TV audience is given as **21 million** by Tennis.com and **23 million** elsewhere. [Tennis.com][tc-wct72], [Tennis.com TBT][tc-wct72b]
- **1973 Davis Cup:** pros were admitted for the first time. Laver returned for the first time since
  1962. Final in **Cleveland** (Public Auditorium): Australia beat the USA **5–0**. Laver, 35, beat Tom
  Gorman **8–10, 8–6, 6–8, 6–3, 6–1**, then clinched the Cup in doubles with John Newcombe over Stan
  Smith and Erik van Dillen. That made five Davis Cup winning teams for Laver. ✅ [Tennis.com][tc-dc73], [Wikipedia][wp-dc73]
- Davis Cup record often given as **20–4**. ⚠️ (one search summary; verify on daviscup.com)
- **1976:** retired from the main tour at 38. Played World TeamTennis for the **San Diego Friars**
  (1976–78) and was named WTT **Rookie of the Year in 1976**. ⚠️ [Britannica via search][brit], [Encyclopedia.com][enc]

## Playing style and equipment

- Wristy topspin off both sides, which was an innovation in the 1960s. The attacking **topspin lob** was
  also a weapon. ⚠️ [Tennis Abstract][ta128]
- Left wrist measured at **7 in** and forearm at **12 in** around (1968). Rosewall called the wrist
  "iron clad". ⚠️ [Wikipedia][wp]
- Won both Grand Slams with **Dunlop Maxply** wooden racquets. ⚠️ [Dunlop][dunlop]

## Life after tennis

- Married **Mary Benson** on **20 June 1966** in San Rafael, California. They met at the Jack Kramer
  Club. She died in **November 2012**, aged 84, at their home in **Carlsbad, California**. ✅ [SI][si-mary], [Tennis Australia][ta-mary]
- Suffered a **stroke on 27 July 1998**, aged 59, while taping an ESPN interview. He was treated at UCLA
  Medical Center and faced a long rehabilitation. ✅ [Washington Post][wapo-stroke], [CBS][cbs-stroke], [Deseret News][des-stroke]

## Era context (for section 4)

- **Amateur vs pro split:** before April 1968 the majors and Davis Cup were amateur-only. Pros played
  their own tours and "pro majors" (US Pro, Wembley, French Pro). ✅
- **Open Era** from 22 April 1968 (Bournemouth). ✅
- **Prize money:** Wimbledon 1968 men's champion £2,000. US Open 1969 winner $16,000 (⚠️). Laver's
  1971 season $292,717. ✅/⚠️ as above.
- **Equipment:** wooden racquets (Dunlop Maxply). Grass for three of the four majors. ⚠️
- **Pros and Davis Cup:** pros first allowed in 1973. ✅ [Tennis.com][tc-dc73]
- World context for the chapters' "era notes" isn't researched yet. I'll only add widely documented
  events (e.g. Apollo 11 in July 1969) with a source for each, once you approve the approach.

## Proposed Journey outline (10 sets)

1. **Set 1: Roots** (1938–1954). Rockhampton, tennis family, cut-down racquet, Charlie Hollis
2. **Set 2: The Hopman Years** (1954–1958). Courier-Mail camp, "Rocket", junior titles ❗year
3. **Set 3: Breakthrough** (1959–1960). Three Wimbledon finals in 1959, first major (AUS 1960 from two sets down)
4. **Set 4: Wimbledon Champion** (1961). d. McKinley, Davis Cup
5. **Set 5: The First Slam** (1962). Four finals, match point saved in Paris
6. **Set 6: Exile** (1963–1966). Turning pro, beaten by Hoad and Rosewall, then rising to the top
7. **Set 7: King of the Pros** (1967). Pro Slam, Wimbledon Pro on Centre Court
8. **Set 8: Open Doors** (1968). Bournemouth, French final loss, first Open Wimbledon
9. **Set 9: The Second Slam** (1969). Roche in the heat, Lall, spikes at Forest Hills
10. **Set 10: Millionaire and Captain's Return** (1970–1976). $1M, WCT Dallas, 1973 Davis Cup, WTT
11. **Set 11: Legacy** (1977–present). Stroke and recovery, Mary, the Arena, the Laver Cup

## Family and childhood (1943–1960)

- Parents **Mattie Cunningham Ashe** and **Arthur Robert Ashe Sr.**, a middle-class Black family in
  segregated Richmond. ✅ [Encyclopedia Virginia][ev], [Library of Virginia][lva]
- When he was four, the family moved into a house at **Brook Field** playground, Richmond's largest
  recreational facility for Black residents. His father was its supervisor. ✅ [Encyclopedia Virginia][ev]
- His **mother died when he was six**, at 28, of a stroke caused by undiagnosed hypertensive vascular
  disease. ⚠️ (the cause and her age are from one source) [Encyclopedia Virginia][ev]
- About a year later he watched **Ronald Charity**, a Virginia Union University student, practising at
  Brook Field's courts. Charity became his first teacher. ✅ [Encyclopedia Virginia][ev], [Library of Virginia][lva]
- Coached from **1953 (age 10) to 1960** at the Lynchburg summer camp of **Dr. Robert Walter Johnson**,
  the physician-coach who had also developed Althea Gibson. ✅ [Encyclopedia Virginia][ev], [ATP bio][atp-bio]
- In **1960**, after three years at Maggie L. Walker High School, he moved to **St. Louis** for his
  senior year under coach **Richard Hudlin**. He graduated from **Sumner High School** (first in his
  class ⚠️). ✅ [Encyclopedia Virginia][ev], [St. Louis Sports HOF][stl]
- **1961:** won the **US Interscholastic** title for Sumner, after Dr. Johnson lobbied for him to be
  admitted to the formerly segregated event. ⚠️ [JRank/search summary][jrank]
- National Junior Indoor title. ❗ Year unclear: a search summary said 1962, other accounts say earlier.
  Needs a primary check. [New World Encyclopedia][nwe]

## UCLA and the Davis Cup (1961–1966)

- Went to **UCLA** on a tennis scholarship. Coached by **J. D. Morgan**, with guidance from
  **Pancho Gonzales**. ✅ [Encyclopedia Virginia][ev], [UCLA Hall of Fame][ucla-hof]
- **1963:** first Black player named to the **US Davis Cup team**. ✅ [ITHF][ithf], [Encyclopedia Virginia][ev]
- **1965:** won the **NCAA singles** title, reportedly d. Mike Belkin (Miami) 6–4, 6–1, 6–1 ⚠️. Also won
  the NCAA doubles with Ian Crookenden, and **UCLA won the team title**. ✅ (re-check: score confirmed by UCLA HOF / SI Vault search results) [Encyclopedia Virginia][ev], [Wikipedia 1965 NCAA][wp-ncaa65]
- **Australian Championships finals, lost both to Roy Emerson:**
  - 1966: 6–4, 6–8, 6–2, 6–3 ⚠️ [Wikipedia][wp-1966a]
  - 1967: 6–4, 6–1, 6–4 ⚠️ [Wikipedia][wp-1967a]

## Army service (1966–1969)

- Entered the **US Army on 4 August 1966** and was commissioned a second lieutenant in the Adjutant
  General's Corps. ⚠️ [Veteran Tributes][vt]
- Posted to the **US Military Academy at West Point** as a data-processing officer. He also ran the
  academy's tennis programme. ✅ West Point / data processing [UCLA Newsroom][ucla-johnnie], [NYPL Ashe archive][nypl] / ⚠️ tennis programme
- Promoted to first lieutenant **23 Feb 1968**. Discharged **1969**. ⚠️ [Veteran Tributes][vt]
- His brother **Johnnie Ashe**, a Marine, volunteered for a second Vietnam tour. According to UCLA's
  account, this is what kept Arthur from being sent to Vietnam. ⚠️ [UCLA Newsroom][ucla-johnnie]

## 1968: the breakthrough (major #1)

- **US Amateur Championships, Longwood Cricket Club, Boston:** d. Bob Lutz **4–6, 6–3, 8–10, 6–0, 6–4**. ⚠️ [ITHF via search][ithf]
- **US Open (first Open-era US Championships), Forest Hills:** d. **Tom Okker 14–12, 5–7, 6–3, 3–6, 6–3**.
  ✅ [Wikipedia][wp-1968u], [NPR][npr], [USOpen.org 50-for-50][uso-50]
  - First Black man to win a men's singles major. ✅ (seed fact)
  - Still registered as an **amateur**, so he couldn't take the **$14,000** first prize, which went to
    runner-up Okker. Ashe received only a daily expense allowance. ❗ The amount is reported as "$20 per
    day" in one source and differs elsewhere, so don't publish a figure until checked. ✅ the $14,000 went to Okker [Wikipedia][wp-1968u], [NPR][npr]
  - He was an Army lieutenant at the time. ✅
  - Winning streak going into or through the final: **26** straight in one source, **24** in another. ❗ [Mississippi Today][mt], [World Tennis Magazine][wtm-68]
  - Reportedly the only player to win both the US Amateur and the US Open in the same year. ⚠️
- **1968 Davis Cup:** the USA beat holders Australia in the Challenge Round in **Adelaide, 26–28 Dec
  1968**. Captain Donald Dell, with Ashe as the top singles player. ✅ [Wikipedia][wp-dc68], [World Tennis Magazine][wtm-dc68]

## South Africa (1969–1973)

- **1969:** turned professional. Applied for a South African visa to play the South African Open and
  was **refused**. Refused again in **1970**. South Africa's sport minister, Frank Waring, cited his
  "general antagonism" towards the country. ✅ [NYPL archive][nypl], [UCLA College][ucla-college]
- **March 1970:** South Africa was **expelled from the Davis Cup**. ✅ [South African History Online][saho].
  ⚠️ Some sources link the expulsion specifically to the Ashe visa refusal. Present it as part of the
  wider protest, not as its sole cause.
- **1973:** finally granted a visa. He became the **first Black professional to play the South African
  national championships** (Ellis Park, Johannesburg) and insisted on **unsegregated seating**. He lost
  the singles final to Jimmy Connors **6–4, 7–6, 6–3** and won the doubles with Tom Okker. ✅ [NYPL archive][nypl], [Wikipedia 1973 SA Open][wp-sa73]

## Majors #2 and #3, and the ATP (1970–1975)

- **1970 Australian Open (major #2):** d. Dick Crealy **6–4, 9–7, 6–2**. First Black man to win the
  Australian title. ✅ [Wikipedia][wp-1970a], [ITHF][ithf]
- 1971 Australian Open final: lost to Ken Rosewall 6–1, 7–5, 6–3. ⚠️ [Wikipedia][wp-1971a]
- 1972 US Open final: lost to Ilie Năstase 3–6, 6–3, 6–7, 6–4, 6–3. ⚠️ [Wikipedia][wp-1972u]
- **1972:** among the players who formed the **Association of Tennis Professionals (ATP)** in September
  1972. Cliff Drysdale was the first president. Ashe was **ATP president in 1974** (some sources say
  1974–75). ✅ co-founder / ⚠️ term [Encyclopedia Virginia][ev], [Wikipedia ATP][wp-atp]
- **1975 WCT Finals, Dallas:** d. Björn Borg **3–6, 6–4, 6–4, 6–0**. ⚠️ [Wikipedia][wp-wct75]
- **1975 Wimbledon (major #3), 5 July 1975:** d. **Jimmy Connors 6–1, 6–1, 5–7, 6–4**. First Black man to
  win Wimbledon. Althea Gibson was the first Black champion there, in 1957–58. Widely called a tactical
  masterpiece: Ashe took the pace off the ball and used slice and soft, low balls to deny Connors the
  pace he fed on. ✅ [ATP Heritage][atp-1975], [ESPN][espn-1975], [Washington Post][wapo-1975], [Tennis.com][tc-1975]
  - Background tension: Connors was then suing Ashe, among others, over ATP matters. I **did not
    confirm** this in this round, so it stays out until sourced.
- **Ranking:** some sources call Ashe **world No. 1 in 1975**. His **ATP computer peak was No. 2
  (10 May 1976)**. ❗ Show both and explain that ATP's ranking and the journalists' year-end
  rankings differ. [ATP bio][atp-bio], [New World Encyclopedia][nwe]

## Heart attack, retirement, captaincy (1977–1985)

- Married photographer **Jeanne Moutoussamy** on **20 Feb 1977** in a UN chapel in New York. Andrew Young
  officiated. ⚠️ [Wikipedia: Jeanne Moutoussamy-Ashe][wp-jeanne], [HistoryMakers][hm]
- **Heart attack: 31 July 1979**. **Quadruple bypass: December 1979**. ✅ [ITHF][ithf], [Library of Virginia][lva]
- **Retired from competition on 16 April 1980.** ✅ [CNN][cnn-retire]
- **US Davis Cup captain 1981–1985.** The USA won the Cup in **1981 and 1982**. He resigned on **22 Oct 1985**. ✅ [Tennis Majors][tm-dc], [ATP bio][atp-bio]
- Davis Cup record as a player: **27–5 in singles**, 1–1 in doubles, over 1963–78; third among US players behind McEnroe (41) and Agassi (30) in the USTA 2019 record book. ✅ (re-check) [ATP bio][atp-bio], [USTA record book PDF](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/pdfs/Davis_Cup_Record_Book_2019_Finals.pdf)
- **Second bypass operation, 1983.** Doctors believed this was when he was infected with HIV through a
  **blood transfusion**. ✅ [Mayo Clinic Proceedings][mayo], [UPI 1992][upi-aids]
- **11 Jan 1985:** **arrested** at an anti-apartheid protest outside the South African embassy in Washington, DC. ✅ [Washington Post][wapo-1985], [Google Arts & Culture arrest citation][gac]
- **1985:** inducted into the **International Tennis Hall of Fame**. ✅ [ITHF][ithf], [BlackPast][bp]
- **Dec 1986:** adopted daughter **Camera**. ⚠️ [Wikipedia][wp-jeanne]

## Historian, AIDS advocate, final years (1988–1993)

- **1988:** published ***A Hard Road to Glory: A History of the African-American Athlete*** (3 vols,
  Warner Books). ✅ [bibliographic listings][hrtg]
- **1988:** learned he was **HIV-positive** after brain surgery, when tests revealed toxoplasmosis. ✅ [Mayo][mayo], [UPI][upi-aids]
- **8 April 1992:** announced he had AIDS at a press conference, because **USA Today** was preparing to
  report it. ✅ [UPI][upi-aids], [STAT News][stat]
- **1992:** founded the **Arthur Ashe Foundation for the Defeat of AIDS**. Also founded the **Arthur Ashe
  Institute for Urban Health** (⚠️ founding year to confirm). ✅ foundation [Mayo][mayo]
- **9 Sept 1992:** **arrested** outside the White House (Lafayette Park) protesting US policy on
  **Haitian refugees**, one of about 95 people arrested. ✅ [UPI][upi-haiti], [Deseret News][des-haiti]
- **1 Dec 1992 (World AIDS Day):** addressed the **UN General Assembly** as chairman of his foundation. ✅ [UN Photo][un-photo], [UN record A/47/PV.76][un-pv]
- **1992:** *Sports Illustrated* **Sportsman of the Year**. ✅ [ITHF via search][ithf], [BlackPast][bp]
- **1993:** memoir ***Days of Grace*** (with Arnold Rampersad, Knopf). ✅ [Penguin Random House][prh]
- **6 Feb 1993:** died. **Lay in state at the Virginia Governor's Mansion**, the first person to do so
  since Stonewall Jackson in 1863. More than 5,000 people filed past. ⚠️ [Legacy.com / search][legacy]

## Proposed Journey outline (11 sets)

1. **Set 1: Brook Field** (1943–1950). Richmond, the playground house, losing his mother
2. **Set 2: Lessons** (1950–1960). Ron Charity, Dr. Johnson's Lynchburg camp
3. **Set 3: St. Louis** (1960–1961). Hudlin, Sumner, the Interscholastic title
4. **Set 4: Bruin** (1961–1966). UCLA, Davis Cup 1963, NCAA 1965, two Australian finals
5. **Set 5: Lieutenant** (1966–1968). The Army, West Point, Johnnie's choice
6. **Set 6: Forest Hills** (1968). US Amateur, US Open as an amateur, the Davis Cup in Adelaide
7. **Set 7: The Wall** (1969–1973). South African visa refusals, Davis Cup expulsion, the 1973 trip
8. **Set 8: Melbourne and the Union** (1970–1974). AO 1970, ATP founding, president
9. **Set 9: The Masterpiece** (1975). WCT Dallas, the Wimbledon final vs Connors
10. **Set 10: Heart** (1977–1985). Marriage, heart attack, retirement, captaincy, arrests, Hall of Fame
11. **Set 11: Days of Grace** (1988–1993). HIV, the announcement, Haiti protest, the UN, death
12. **Set 12: Legacy** (1993–). Medal of Freedom, monument, stadium, the Courage Award
